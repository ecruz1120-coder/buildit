Mobile Park Gym: Concept Schematics

Concept for a 12 ft box truck. Dimensions are typical for the class and drawn to scale in inches. Measure your actual truck before cutting anything. These are concept drawings for discussion with a fabricator and a structural engineer. They are not stamped engineering drawings.

Concerns to settle first.
  • The roof is structural. A box body gets much of its rigidity from the roof tying the two walls together. Cutting it off needs a steel header ring and wall uprights before anything else, or the body will rack and the hinge will tear out.
  • The lid is a sail. A 92 sq ft flat canopy sees hundreds of pounds of wind uplift in a Phoenix monsoon gust. It needs ballasted posts and a hard rule to close it in wind.
  • Hanging people from it is the high-risk part. Ask the insurer whether they will write overhead hanging loads and a climbing wall before you build.
  • A flip-over lid is heavy. A steel-framed lid that can carry people weighs about 450 lb. Plan for powered or counterbalanced actuation plus manual backup, not muscle.

Design in one paragraph

The box keeps its floor, walls and rear gate. The roof becomes a separate steel-framed lid hinged along the curb side. On the road it sits flush, gasketed and latched. At the park it swings 180 degrees to the curb side and lands horizontal at the top of the wall, where three ballasted telescoping posts hold its outer edge. That makes a shaded, open-air training bay with a rig hanging from the lid. The rear gate drops to a horizontal platform. The truck stays on pavement and gear goes onto the grass or mats.

Choices I made, change any of them

  • Hinge on the curb side. The canopy deploys away from traffic. If you meant the other side, mirror the drawings.
  • Lid is flipped, so its outside face points down. The rig mounts to flush T-slot rails on the lid's top surface, and those rails hang under the canopy once it flips. On the road the rails sit flush on top.
  • Drop-gate platform replaces a roll-up door. Option 2 uses two swing-out leaves that act as climbing walls at 90 degrees.

A. Travel configuration

Everything folds inside the legal envelope. The lid is flush with the header ring, latched on five points, and the posts ride inside the box.

B. Deployed, from the rear

The box is open to the sky with the lid flipped. Gear on the slatwall and shelves is exposed, so stow it before rain. Rig height shown is 96 in to the bar.

C. Deployed, from above

The footprint is about 15 ft 4 in wide and 26 ft long before mats. Check the parking space or pad size you need against what each park allows.

D. Deployed, from the curb side

The gate platform doubles as a box-jump stage. Cable stops and two fold-down legs carry it. Keep the license plate and rear lights visible or add repeater lights.

E. Hinge and header detail

The header ring is the part that makes the cut safe. The hinge, actuators and lock pins all load into it, not into the thin wall skin.

Gusts run well above sustained speed in a monsoon, so the 20 mph operating cap is a floor, not a margin. Ballast on each post must exceed the uplift share with a safety factor, and the engineer should size it.

Deploy and stow sequence (target: two people, about 10 minutes)

  1. Park on level pavement with the curb side toward the park. Chock wheels, set the parking brake, switch off the engine.
  2. Check wind and the forecast. Stop here above 20 mph sustained or with storms in the area.
  3. Drop the gate. Set the two fold-down legs and attach the cable stops.
  4. Pull the posts and base plates from the box floor. Set the base plates and ballast on the curb side.
  5. Release the five lid latches. Pull the road-side lock pin.
  6. Run the actuators to raise the lid through 180 degrees. Watch the whole swing and keep people clear.
  7. Insert the lock pin at 180 degrees. Pin the telescoping posts to height and fasten them to the lid.
  8. Hang the rig. Inspect straps, bolts and pins using the daily checklist. Log it.
  9. To stow, reverse the order. Unhang the rig first. Never fold the lid with anything hanging from it.

Cheaper and lower-risk alternative worth pricing

Keep the box roof. Mount a 14 ft engineered fold-out rig frame on the curb side that stows against the wall, with an awning for shade. You lose the open-roof idea but keep the roof's structure, the gasket, and the insurance conversation stays simple. Get a quote for both before you cut anything.

Split lid variant

Instead of one 92 in lid, make two 46 in leaves that flip to opposite sides. Each leaf weighs about half, needs about a quarter of the torque, and presents less wind area. The cost is a smaller canopy on each side and a center beam. It deploys on both sides, so the traffic side of the truck is exposed. Use it only where the driver side faces a barrier or the lot is closed.

Next steps

  2. Measure the truck: box inside and outside dimensions, floor height, wall thickness, GVWR sticker.
  3. Hire a structural engineer to review the header ring, hinge, posts and wind load. A fabricator can quote from this page.
  4. Build a half-scale lid or a plywood mock-up to test the swing and setup time before cutting steel.
