# Mobile Park Gym: Legal and Permit Research

Concept: a 10 ft or 12 ft box truck with the roof cut off as a hinged lid that flips 180 degrees into a canopy, a rear door that becomes a platform or climbing wall, and the truck body itself as the gym and equipment storage. Target area is the West Valley plus parts of Phoenix and the east side.

Date of research: 2026-10-26. Assumed readings of your message: "Tollison" is Tolleson, "avindale" is Avondale, and "park war gym" is a park workout gym.

Nothing here is legal advice. Before you spend real money on the build, have an Arizona attorney review the liability section and an engineer review the structure.

## Part 1. Is the truck itself legal to modify and drive?

### 1.1 Size limits on public roads

| Item | Limit | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Height | 13 ft 6 in without a permit. | [Verified] [A.R.S. 28-1094](https://azleg.gov/ars/28/01094.htm) |
| Width | Search results conflict: 8 ft in one reading, 102 in (8 ft 6 in) on designated routes in another. | [Secondary] See [azbikelaw.org summary](https://azbikelaw.org/width-of-vehicles/) |
| Length | 40 ft for a single vehicle. | [Secondary] [Freight Sidekick](https://freightsidekick.com/resources/state-dot-regulations/arizona) |

What it means for your design:

- A 10 or 12 ft box truck is about 10 ft 8 in tall and 8 ft wide, so you have roughly 2.5 ft of height to spare.
- **Everything must fold inside the stowed envelope.** Hinges, latches, clamp brackets and rails cannot stick out past the body width while driving.
- Anything bolted to the top of the lid, such as flush T-slot rails, adds to height. Keep it low.
- Never drive with the lid up, the platform down, or the posts attached.

### 1.2 Registration, weight class and title

- A converted vehicle used as a business unit is generally registered commercially. A Mesa mobile-vendor document says the unit itself needs commercial registration and a towing vehicle only personal registration. That is a food-truck document, so it is only a hint. [Secondary] [Mesa](https://mesaaz.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/business-development/licensing/important-documents-mobile-food-vendors.pdf)
- An old forum claim says over 8,500 lb GVWR requires commercial plates. [Secondary, dated 2003, do not rely on it]
- Forum posts describe MVD disputes when a heavy truck is retitled to a different body type. Expect questions if you change the body type on record. [Secondary]
- Federal and state class thresholds I am carrying from background knowledge. [Background]
  - Under 10,001 lb GVWR: generally no USDOT number needed for intrastate use and no CDL.
  - 10,001 to 26,000 lb: federal rules may require a USDOT number if you cross state lines. Arizona intrastate rules differ.
  - 26,001 lb and up: CDL.
- A 10 ft box truck on a Ford E-350 or similar cutaway is usually near 9,500 to 12,500 lb GVWR. A 12 ft box on an Isuzu NPR class chassis is usually about 12,000 to 14,500 lb. Read the door-jamb sticker on the actual truck.

**Action:** Call or visit ADOT Motor Vehicle Division and ask three things. What plate class applies to this GVWR when used for a fitness business? Does cutting and re-hinging the body require re-recording the body type? Is a USDOT number required for purely intrastate use at this weight?

### 1.3 Equipment that must stay working

[Background] Removing the roof hits several federal required items. Federal rules bar businesses from making required safety equipment inoperative, and a roof removal tends to disturb exactly these:

- **Clearance and identification lamps.** Trucks 80 in wide or more need amber clearance lamps at the top front corners and rear identification lamps, which live on the roof header. Relocate them to the fixed frame, not the lid.
- **Reflective conspicuity tape** on the sides and rear.
- **Rear impact guard** (the bumper bar) on heavy trucks.
- **Rear lights and license plate** must not be hidden when the rear gate is down. A gate that drops down must carry repeater lights or the lights must stay visible. Keep this in mind for the platform design.
- Arizona does not run periodic safety inspections on private vehicles. Metro Phoenix does run emissions testing. Check whether your truck's age and weight class fall in it. [Background]

### 1.4 Insurance

- A standard commercial auto policy will not automatically cover a structurally altered body with moving parts. Tell the insurer exactly what you built, in writing, before you run it. An undisclosed modification is a common reason for a denied claim.
- You need two policies: commercial auto for the truck, and commercial general liability for the training business. Cities almost always require the general liability policy and ask to be named as additional insured. A Texas example shows the typical structure: one million dollars in general liability with the city as additional insured. [Secondary] [Fate TX policy](https://www.fatetx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2098/Fitness-Class-Use-Policy)
- Ask the insurer plainly whether they will cover a climbing wall and overhead hanging loads. Many will exclude them. This may decide the design.

### 1.5 Structure and safety are not legal-optional

There is no Arizona permit I found that approves a hand-built folding structure. That does not make it safe, and it does not protect you from a lawsuit. Sections 2.5 and 3 cover this.

### 2.3 The typical rule pattern, from other cities

Because the Arizona cities did not turn up, here is the pattern other US cities use. Expect something like it. This is **not** Arizona law.

- Permit required once you charge money, and often once a group exceeds a small number, sometimes four. [Secondary] [Fate TX](https://www.fatetx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2098/Fitness-Class-Use-Policy)
- Annual or per-session fee, sometimes a percentage of what clients pay.
- One million dollars general liability with the city named as additional insured.
- You must not block public use, entrances or fire lanes.
- Instructor-to-participant ratio limits, and bans on amplified sound.
- Limits on the number of locations per permit.

### 2.4 Grass, fields and ground contact

- [Background] City codes usually forbid driving motor vehicles on turf and landscaped areas outside roads and parking lots. Plan on the truck staying in the lot or on a paved pad. This is the biggest design effect: your canopy and platform must work from pavement, with gear carried onto grass.
- [Background] Many parks forbid driving stakes because of buried irrigation lines. Design the posts for ballast, such as water barrels or sandbags on base plates, not stakes. If you ever need to dig or stake deeply on open ground, call Arizona 811 first.
- Fields are often controlled by the city's field allocation program or by a school district, not by general park rules. [Secondary] Phoenix has a field allocation process on its parks permits page.

### 2.5 Heat, wind and weather are legal issues too

- Monsoon gusts exceed 60 mph. A lid acting as a canopy is a big sail. The numbers are in `schematics.html`. A reasonable operating rule is to close or lower the lid when sustained wind reaches about 20 mph or storm warnings are issued. Put that in your written safety policy, because it is the first thing a plaintiff's lawyer or an insurer will ask.
- Heat: keep water, shade and a first-aid kit and AED on board. That is cheap risk control and often an insurer's condition.

## Part 3. Business, tax and liability

### 3.1 Licenses

- **Transaction privilege tax (TPT) license.** Arizona requires registration with the Department of Revenue before any taxable activity, through AZTaxes.gov. [Verified] [ADOR](https://www.azdor.gov/business) Gym and fitness facility membership fees can fall under the amusements class. Whether pay-per-session personal training is taxable is **unresolved**. Ask ADOR for a written answer, or check Publication 604 and A.R.S. 42-5073. [Secondary]
- **City business license** in each city you operate or are based in. Peoria requires one for mobile units. [Verified for Peoria]
- Arizona Commerce Authority has a fitness business checklist. [Secondary] [azcommerce.com](https://www.azcommerce.com/small-business/checklist-items/medicalhealthcare-related-business-activity/health-clubgymyoga-studiofitness-center)
- Food or drink sales, if ever, trigger county health permits and fire inspection. Skip them at first.

### 3.2 Liability

- [Background, verify with an attorney] Arizona's constitution (Article 18, Section 5) makes assumption of risk a jury question. Courts have treated signed releases as weaker here than in many states. A waiver helps. It does not make you safe.
- Heavier-risk features, in order: people hanging from the canopy lid, the climbing wall, box jumps and a raised platform, then ordinary floor work. Decide which you will offer to the public.
- Keep a written daily inspection log for hinges, pins, cables and bolts. It is the best evidence you can have.
- ADA: a business open to the public has obligations for accessible routes and service. [Background] Keep it in mind when choosing the platform height and ramp.

### 3.3 Hiring or training standards

Fitness trainers in Arizona are not state-licensed. [Background] Insurers and parks departments often want a recognized certification such as NASM, ACE or NSCA, plus CPR and AED certification. Climbing instruction may need its own training.

## Part 4. Call list, in order

1. Insurance broker: will they write commercial auto for the altered truck and general liability with climbing and overhead loads? **Do this first. It can end the project cheaply.**
2. ADOT MVD: plate class, body-type change, USDOT number at your GVWR.
3. Arizona Department of Revenue: TPT license and whether training sessions are taxable.
4. Parks departments of each city, one at a time, starting with Peoria and Phoenix: commercial-use permit, fees, insurance, location limits, whether a vehicle can enter the lot.
5. Maricopa County Parks: if you want regional parks.
6. A structural engineer (Arizona PE): hinge, lid frame, posts, wind, and any hanging loads.
7. An Arizona attorney: waiver, entity formation (LLC), and a review of the permit terms.
